# Phase 8 — Cybersecurity Job Application

## Goal of this phase

Apply for realistic entry-level cyber roles using your IT experience, portfolio, and targeted specialization.

## Estimated time

**8–16 weeks alongside work/study**. Cyber searches can take longer than IT searches.

## Skills you'll gain

- Target SOC, junior GRC, IT security, and junior pentest roles appropriately.
- Tailor a cyber resume using evidence.
- Explain labs without pretending they are professional experience.
- Network professionally and prepare for technical screens.

## Target roles

Primary targets:

- SOC Analyst L1
- Security Operations Trainee
- Junior Security Analyst
- IT Security Analyst
- GRC Analyst Junior
- Security Compliance Assistant
- Vulnerability Management Assistant
- Technical Support with security responsibilities

Use IT support as a bridge, not a failure. Real account, endpoint, network, and ticket experience is valuable.

## Hands-on practice tasks

1. Create separate SOC, GRC, and IT security resume versions, then choose one primary.
2. Add 3–5 projects with links and one-line business impact.
3. Apply to 5–10 targeted roles/week.
4. Track keywords appearing in job descriptions.
5. Practice technical questions: DNS, TCP, Linux permissions, Windows Event IDs, phishing triage, SIEM alert triage, MFA, vulnerability prioritization.
6. Practice explaining one project in 2 minutes and 10 minutes.
7. Ask for referrals politely after building a genuine professional connection.

## Deliverable / proof of work

- Cyber resume
- Updated LinkedIn
- GitHub/portfolio with 3–5 reports
- Application tracker
- 30-question interview bank
- 3 project explanations

## Checklist

- [ ] I chose a primary cyber role family.
- [ ] I created a targeted cyber resume.
- [ ] I linked 3–5 completed projects.
- [ ] I updated LinkedIn/GitHub.
- [ ] I created an application tracker.
- [ ] I applied to at least 50 targeted roles over time.
- [ ] I practiced technical and behavioral interviews.
- [ ] I honestly separate labs from professional experience.

## You're ready to move on when...

You can explain your transition from IT to cyber, walk through a project with evidence, and answer basic technical questions calmly.

## Free vs Paid

### What's free and enough

Job boards, GitHub, Google Sheets, LinkedIn free, and free interview practice are enough.

### What's paid and why you'd upgrade

LinkedIn Premium or paid resume reviews can add convenience, but they do not guarantee interviews.

### When it's worth paying

Only consider paid services after you have a stable resume and evidence that applications are the bottleneck.
