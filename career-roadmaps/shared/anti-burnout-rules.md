# Anti-Burnout Rules

This roadmap is intentionally built for someone who can study consistently but burns out when the plan becomes too big.

## Minimum Effective Study Day

A successful day can be only:

- 25 minutes learning
- 25 minutes hands-on practice
- 10 minutes notes/checklist update

That is enough. The goal is not intensity. The goal is not quitting.

## Weekly Limits

- Target: **10–15 hours/week**.
- Maximum: **20 hours/week** only for rare short bursts.
- Take at least **1 full rest day/week**.
- If you miss a day, do not double the next day. Restart normally.

## The 3-Level Day System

| Energy Level | What to Do |
|---|---|
| Low | Watch one short lesson, review notes, or update checklist |
| Normal | Learn one topic and complete one mini-task |
| High | Build a lab, write a portfolio item, or apply to jobs |

## Visible Wins Rule

Every week should produce one visible artifact:

- Screenshot of a VM
- Ticket template
- Network diagram
- Troubleshooting write-up
- Wireshark capture screenshot
- Resume bullet
- LinkedIn update
- SIEM alert screenshot
- Incident report

## Burnout Warning Signs

Reduce workload for one week if:

- You keep rereading the same lesson but retain nothing.
- You feel guilty instead of curious.
- You avoid the roadmap for 3+ days because it feels too big.
- You jump between tools without finishing tasks.

## Recovery Protocol

For 5 study days:

1. Study only 30–45 minutes/day.
2. Do review tasks only.
3. No new tools, no new courses, no paid subscriptions.
4. End each session by writing: `Today I completed ____.`

## Hiring-Manager Reality Check

For entry-level roles, reliable documentation and calm troubleshooting often beat raw memorization. You do not need to know everything. You need to show that you can learn, document, ask good questions, and not panic.
