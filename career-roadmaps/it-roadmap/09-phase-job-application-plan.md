# Phase 9 — Job Application Plan

## Goal of this phase

Apply consistently to remote entry-level IT roles while improving your resume and interview performance from feedback.

## Estimated time

**8–12 weeks alongside study**. Some people land faster, but beginners without a degree should plan for a longer search.

## Skills you'll gain

- Search for realistic entry-level roles.
- Customize resume keywords without lying.
- Track applications and follow-ups.
- Prepare for screening calls and technical interviews.
- Avoid scams and low-quality job traps.

## Target roles

Prioritize:

- Helpdesk Technician
- IT Support Specialist
- Service Desk Analyst
- Technical Support Representative with IT products
- Desktop Support, remote/hybrid if possible
- NOC Technician trainee/junior
- Junior System Administrator assistant
- SaaS Technical Support Specialist

Avoid as first targets unless clearly junior:

- Security Engineer
- Penetration Tester
- Cloud Security Engineer
- Senior Sysadmin
- DevOps Engineer

## Hands-on practice tasks

1. Create an application tracker with columns: Company, Role, Link, Date, Resume Version, Status, Follow-up, Notes.
2. Apply to 5–10 roles/week at first; increase to 15/week once resume is stable.
3. Customize the top third of your resume for each role type.
4. Prepare answers for: Tell me about yourself, why IT, no degree explanation, difficult user, troubleshooting example.
5. Practice 20 technical questions: DNS, DHCP, IP, VPN, password reset, MFA, Windows logs, Linux permissions.
6. Send 5 polite LinkedIn connection requests/week to IT recruiters or support leads.
7. Review failed applications every 2 weeks and adjust keywords.

## Deliverable / proof of work

- Application tracker with at least 50 entries over time
- Interview answer document
- 2 resume versions: Helpdesk/IT Support and NOC/Networking Support
- List of 20 technical Q&A

## You're ready to move on when...

You are actively applying every week, improving based on feedback, and can explain your labs confidently in interviews.

## Free vs Paid

### What's free and enough

All listed job boards are free for applicants. Google Sheets and Google Calendar are enough.

### What's paid and why you'd upgrade

LinkedIn Premium can show extra applicant insights, but it does not replace skills, portfolio, or volume.

### When it's worth paying

Do not pay while budget is $0. If you later have income, consider one month only during heavy job search, but it is optional.
