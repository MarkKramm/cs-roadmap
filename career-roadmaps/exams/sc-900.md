# Microsoft SC-900 — practice questions

**Unofficial.** Written for this repository against Microsoft's published SC-900 study guide.
Not real exam questions, not endorsed by Microsoft.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Exam code** | SC-900 |
| **Real exam** | approximately 50 questions, 45 minutes |
| **Pass mark** | **700** on a scale of 1000 |
| **This paper** | **40 questions** — see the note below |

## Before you start

**Do this closed-book, in one sitting, in 36 minutes.** The real exam is about 45 minutes for
roughly 50 questions, which is **under a minute per question** — the tightest pace of any paper
in this directory. Time pressure is a genuine failure mode here: candidates who know the material
still run out of time. 36 minutes for 40 questions matches the real rate.

**700/1000 is 70%**, which is **28 questions** out of 40 — the lowest threshold of the exams
here, and a real one. Microsoft scores on a 1000-point scale and 700 is the pass:

| Your score out of 40 | Percentage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 32–40 | 80–100% | Comfortable pass |
| **28–31** | **70–78%** | **Pass** |
| 27 | 67.5% | Fail — just under the line |
| 24–26 | 60–65% | Clear fail |
| below 24 | — | Substantially under-prepared |

**Do not read "70%" as easy.** SC-900 is a **fundamentals** exam: it is broad rather than deep,
and it requires knowing Microsoft's specific product names and what each one does. People fail it
by knowing security in general and not knowing which Microsoft product does which job.

**Microsoft does not publish exact question counts.** The study guide gives the skills measured
and their weights; the number of questions and the time limit are approximated from the exam
page and are marked as approximate above. Confirm with Microsoft when you book.

## How it is weighted

Microsoft publishes four domains with **ranges**, not fixed percentages. This paper targets the
midpoint of each range and stays inside it:

| Domain | Official weight | Questions here | Share of this paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Concepts of security, compliance and identity | 10–15% | 5 | 12% |
| 2. Capabilities of Microsoft Entra | 25–30% | 11 | 28% |
| 3. Capabilities of Microsoft security solutions | **35–40%** | 15 | **38%** |

for Cloud, Defender for Identity, Sentinel, Purview — reading general security material will not
cover it.

## Domain 1 — Concepts of security, compliance and identity (10–15%)

### Q1. What does the shared responsibility model describe?

- [ ] Who owns the hardware in a datacentre
- [x] **Which security responsibilities belong to the cloud provider and which remain with the customer**
- [ ] How costs are shared between departments
- [ ] Which employees may access the cloud

**Why:** The model divides **security duties** and the split moves with the service type — the
customer's share is largest in IaaS and smallest in SaaS. This is foundational to everything else
in SC-900, and Microsoft frames the whole exam around it. It is about responsibilities, not
about cost, hardware ownership, or user permissions.

### Q2. Which concept requires verification through two or more different types of evidence?

- [ ] Single sign-on
- [x] **Multifactor authentication**
- [ ] Conditional Access
- [ ] Passwordless authentication

**Why:** Multifactor authentication requires factors of **different types** — something you know,
have, or are. Note that passwordless is a related but distinct idea: it removes the password
entirely, which often *uses* MFA technology but is not the same concept. Conditional Access is
the policy engine that decides *when* to demand authentication, not the authentication itself —
a distinction the exam tests.

### Q3. What is the principle of least privilege?

- [ ] Granting all users administrator rights for convenience
- [x] **Granting only the access necessary to perform a task**
- [ ] Requiring long passwords
- [ ] Encrypting all data at rest

**Why:** Least privilege limits **access** to the minimum needed. It appears in Microsoft's
guidance as the reason for Privileged Identity Management (just-in-time admin access) and for
role-based access control — both of which exist specifically to avoid standing administrator
rights. It is not about password length or encryption, which address different risks.

### Q4. What is the purpose of a data classification scheme?

- [ ] To compress data
- [x] **To label data by sensitivity so that appropriate protection can be applied**
- [ ] To back up data
- [ ] To encrypt data

**Why:** Classification exists so protection can be **proportionate** — public material and
customer records should not be handled identically. In Microsoft's world this appears as
sensitivity labels in Purview, which is why the concept sits at the front of the exam: the
labels are the mechanism, classification is the idea. Encryption and backup are separate
controls that may *follow* from a classification decision.

### Q5. What is the difference between authentication and authorization?

- [ ] They are the same thing
- [x] **Authentication proves who you are; authorization determines what you may do**
- [ ] Authentication is for users; authorization is for devices
- [ ] Authorization happens first

**Why:** Authentication comes **first** and answers "who are you"; authorization answers "what
are you allowed to do" and depends on the first. Keeping them separate matters in practice:
an account can be correctly authenticated and still be denied access, and troubleshooting
requires knowing which of the two failed. Option three is wrong on both counts.

## Domain 2 — Capabilities of Microsoft Entra (25–30%)

### Q6. What is Microsoft Entra ID?

- [ ] A firewall product
- [x] **Microsoft's cloud-based identity and access management service**
- [ ] A backup service
- [ ] An email server

**Why:** **Entra ID** is the identity platform — the successor name for Azure Active Directory.
It is where users, groups, devices and applications are managed, and it is the thing that
authenticates sign-ins to Microsoft 365 and thousands of other apps. It is not a firewall or a
backup service; it is the identity layer everything else builds on.

### Q7. A company wants its employees to use their existing on-premises Active Directory credentials to sign in to cloud applications. What should they implement?

- [ ] A separate cloud password for each user
- [x] **Microsoft Entra Connect, to synchronise identities to Entra ID**
- [ ] A VPN for every user
- [ ] Multifactor authentication only

**Why:** **Entra Connect** synchronises on-premises identities into Entra ID, which is what
delivers a single set of credentials across both. Requiring a second, separate cloud password is
the problem this solves — and it is what organisations do before they implement sync. A VPN
provides network access, not identity federation.

### Q8. What does Microsoft Entra ID Protection do?

- [ ] Blocks all sign-ins from outside the country
- [x] **Detects and reports risky users and risky sign-ins, and can act on them automatically**
- [ ] Encrypts files on user devices
- [ ] Manages software licences

**Why:** **ID Protection** is the risk-detection layer — it identifies signals like leaked
credentials, impossible travel and atypical sign-in patterns, and can feed those into
Conditional Access to require MFA or block the sign-in. This is behaviour-based detection applied
to identity. It is not a blanket geographic block, which would be a blunt and easily broken rule.

### Q9. What is the purpose of Conditional Access in Entra ID?

- [ ] To store user passwords securely
- [x] **To enforce access decisions based on signals such as user, device, location and risk**
- [ ] To synchronise on-premises accounts
- [ ] To provide a self-service password reset

**Why:** Conditional Access is an **if-then policy engine** — "if a user is off-network on an
unmanaged device, then require MFA". It is the mechanism that makes zero trust enforceable, which
is why it sits at the centre of Microsoft's identity story. Password storage, sync and self-service
reset are all different features; the exam separates them carefully.

### Q10. What is self-service password reset (SSPR) in Entra ID?

- [ ] An administrator resetting a user's password
- [x] **Allowing users to reset their own passwords after verifying their identity**
- [ ] A password complexity policy
- [ ] Automatic password rotation

**Why:** **SSPR** removes the helpdesk from the reset loop — the user proves their identity and
resets, which reduces both cost and delay. It is one of the highest-value features for a support
team and appears on the exam for that reason. Note it is user-initiated, not administrator-driven,
and it is not a complexity or rotation policy.

### Q11. Which Entra ID feature grants a user administrator rights only for a limited time when needed?

- [ ] A permanent role assignment
- [x] **Privileged Identity Management (PIM)**
- [ ] A security group
- [ ] A shared mailbox

**Why:** **PIM** provides **just-in-time** privileged access — the user requests elevation, gets
it for a defined window, and it expires. This is least privilege (Q3) implemented at scale, and it
directly reduces the danger of standing administrator accounts. A permanent assignment is exactly
what PIM exists to replace.

### Q12. What is the purpose of Entra ID's access reviews?

- [ ] To review software licences
- [x] **To periodically verify that users still need the access they have, and remove it if not**
- [ ] To test password strength
- [ ] To review security incidents

**Why:** Access accumulates — people change roles and keep old permissions, and that drift is how
over-privileged accounts happen. **Access reviews** force a periodic, accountable re-check. This is
governance rather than technical enforcement, and it is the piece organisations skip most often
because nothing breaks immediately when they do.

### Q13. What is a key difference between Microsoft Entra ID and on-premises Active Directory Domain Services?

- [ ] Entra ID has no support for groups
- [x] **Entra ID is a cloud identity service with no domain join, forests or Group Policy in the AD DS sense**
- [ ] Entra ID does not support authentication
- [ ] AD DS cannot be used with Microsoft 365

**Why:** The two are related but architecturally different — AD DS is a hierarchical directory with
domains and forests; Entra ID is a flat, cloud-hosted identity service using different protocols
and with its own management tools. People expect Entra ID to be "AD in the cloud" and are surprised
by the differences. Option four is backwards: AD DS is commonly used *with* Microsoft 365 via
synchronisation.

### Q14. Which external identity scenario lets a partner organisation use its own credentials to access your resources?

- [ ] A local account created for each partner user
- [x] **B2B collaboration in Entra External ID**
- [ ] A shared administrator account
- [ ] A VPN account per partner

**Why:** **B2B collaboration** lets guests authenticate with their own home organisation's
credentials, so you do not manage their passwords and they are disabled automatically when they
leave their employer. Creating local accounts for each partner user is the alternative — and it is
the one that leaves orphaned accounts behind. Shared accounts destroy accountability.

### Q15. What does Microsoft Entra Verified ID provide?

- [ ] A password manager
- [x] **Verifiable digital credentials that can be presented without revealing more than necessary**
- [ ] A VPN service
- [ ] An email encryption gateway

**Why:** **Verified ID** issues cryptographically verifiable credentials — a verified employee
badge, a certification — that can be presented as proof without handing over the underlying
documents. The privacy point is the design goal: proving you are over 18 without revealing your
date of birth. It is a newer area and worth recognising by name, which is often all the exam asks.

### Q16. Which Entra ID feature would block a sign-in from a country where the company does not operate?

- [ ] Self-service password reset
- [x] **Conditional Access with a location-based condition, or a named location policy**
- [ ] Access reviews
- [ ] Entra Connect

**Why:** A geographic restriction is a **Conditional Access** rule — the signal is location, and
the action is block. This is Q9 applied, and the exam frequently describes a scenario and asks
which feature implements it. Note that a country block is a blunt control: attackers use
residential proxies in the target country, so it is a layer rather than a solution.

## Domain 3 — Capabilities of Microsoft security solutions (35–40%)

### Q17. What is Microsoft Defender for Cloud?

- [ ] An email filtering service
- [x] **A cloud security posture management and workload protection service for cloud and hybrid resources**
- [ ] A password manager
- [ ] A VPN

**Why:** **Defender for Cloud** assesses your cloud configuration for weaknesses (secure score),
protects workloads, and covers Azure, AWS and GCP. **Secure score** — a measurable posture rating
with prioritised recommendations — is the feature most often asked about by name. It is not email
filtering; that is Defender for Office 365.

### Q18. Which service provides email protection against phishing and malicious attachments in Microsoft 365?

- [ ] Microsoft Defender for Cloud
- [x] **Microsoft Defender for Office 365**
- [ ] Microsoft Sentinel
- [ ] Microsoft Purview

**Why:** **Defender for Office 365** covers email and collaboration threats — phishing, malicious
attachments and links, and Business Email Compromise. The Defender family is deliberately split
by workload, and the exam tests whether you know which covers which: Office 365 for email,
Cloud for cloud infrastructure, Identity for on-premises identity signals, Endpoint for devices.

### Q19. What is Microsoft Sentinel?

- [ ] An endpoint antivirus
- [x] **A cloud-native SIEM and SOAR solution for collecting, detecting, investigating and responding to threats**
- [ ] A backup service
- [ ] An identity provider

**Why:** **Sentinel** is the **SIEM** (security information and event management) plus **SOAR**
(orchestration, automation and response) offering — it ingests logs from across the estate,
correlates them, and can automate responses with playbooks. The two acronyms are worth knowing,
because questions often ask which product provides SIEM. It is not a Defender workload product;
it is where their signals are correlated.

### Q20. Which Microsoft product discovers and protects sensitive information across Microsoft 365, on-premises and cloud apps?

- [ ] Microsoft Defender for Endpoint
- [x] **Microsoft Purview**
- [ ] Microsoft Entra ID
- [ ] Microsoft Intune

**Why:** **Purview** is the data security, governance and compliance family — sensitivity labels,
data loss prevention, and compliance management. The distinction from Defender is worth being
clear on: **Defender protects against attackers, Purview protects the data itself** regardless of
who is touching it. Data loss prevention is about preventing accidental or inappropriate sharing,
not about stopping an intrusion.

### Q21. Which service provides threat protection for devices such as laptops and servers?

- [ ] Microsoft Defender for Cloud
- [x] **Microsoft Defender for Endpoint**
- [ ] Microsoft Sentinel
- [ ] Microsoft Purview

**Why:** **Defender for Endpoint** is the endpoint detection and response product — device-level
protection, investigation and remediation. It is the third of the Defender workloads worth knowing
by name alongside Cloud (Q17) and Office 365 (Q18). Endpoint means the device; the exam will
describe a laptop and expect this answer.

### Q22. What is the purpose of Microsoft Defender for Identity?

- [ ] To protect Azure virtual machines
- [x] **To monitor on-premises Active Directory signals to detect identity-based attacks**
- [ ] To filter email
- [ ] To manage mobile devices

**Why:** **Defender for Identity** watches on-premises AD for reconnaissance and lateral movement
techniques — the identity attacks that precede ransomware. Note the deliberate contrast with
Defender for Endpoint (devices) and Entra ID Protection (cloud identity risk): this one covers
**on-premises identity**. That three-way distinction is a favourite exam question.

### Q23. What does Microsoft Defender for Cloud's secure score represent?

- [ ] The number of threats blocked
- [x] **A measurement of your current security posture against recommended controls, with prioritised improvements**
- [ ] The number of licensed users
- [ ] The age of your resources

**Why:** **Secure score** is a posture **measurement** with recommendations ranked by impact, which
makes it a prioritisation tool rather than a count of incidents. Its value is telling you what to
fix first — the same risk-prioritisation logic as vulnerability management. "Threats blocked" is
an operational metric and a different thing entirely.

### Q24. Which capability allows an organisation to automatically block a sign-in it judges risky?

- [ ] Access reviews
- [x] **Conditional Access with sign-in risk as a condition, informed by ID Protection**
- [ ] Secure score
- [ ] Data loss prevention

**Why:** This combines three products in one scenario — **ID Protection** detects the risk,
**Conditional Access** acts on it — and questions that chain features like this are common. The
answer must name the enforcement mechanism, and only Conditional Access enforces sign-in
decisions. Secure score measures posture; access reviews are periodic governance, not real-time
enforcement.

### Q25. What is Microsoft Defender for Cloud Apps?

- [ ] An antivirus for cloud servers
- [x] **A cloud access security broker (CASB) that discovers and governs the use of cloud applications**
- [ ] A backup service for OneDrive
- [ ] A web hosting platform

**Why:** **Defender for Cloud Apps** is the **CASB** — it discovers which cloud apps staff are
actually using (shadow IT), governs them, and can detect anomalous behaviour within them. The
acronym is worth knowing because the exam uses "CASB" directly. Note the naming trap: "Cloud Apps"
is about SaaS usage, while "Defender for Cloud" (Q17) is about cloud *infrastructure*.

### Q26. What is the purpose of Microsoft Intune?

- [ ] To manage cloud spending
- [x] **To manage and secure devices and applications (MDM and MAM)**
- [ ] To provide a SIEM
- [ ] To classify data

**Why:** **Intune** is mobile device management and mobile application management — enrolling
devices, enforcing compliance, and deploying apps and configuration. It answers "is this device
allowed to access company data, and is it compliant?" Compliance signals from Intune feed into
Conditional Access, which is how device state becomes an access decision. It is not a SIEM, a
cost tool, or a classification tool.

### Q27. Which Microsoft solution helps an organisation meet regulatory requirements by tracking controls and assessments?

- [ ] Microsoft Defender for Endpoint
- [x] **Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager**
- [ ] Microsoft Sentinel
- [ ] Microsoft Entra ID

**Why:** **Compliance Manager** maps your controls to regulations and standards, scores your
progress, and tracks improvement actions. It is the compliance counterpart to secure score. The
pattern to notice is that Microsoft splits **posture measurement** into two products: secure score
for security, Compliance Manager for compliance — and the exam asks which is which.

### Q28. What are Microsoft's three principles of a zero trust strategy?

- [ ] Firewall, antivirus and patching
- [x] **Verify explicitly, use least privilege access, and assume breach**
- [ ] Encrypt, monitor and audit
- [ ] Authenticate, authorize and account

**Why:** These three are stated by Microsoft by name and the exam expects them. **Assume breach**
is the one people find counterintuitive and the most useful — it means designing as though an
attacker is already inside, which is why segmentation and monitoring matter more than a strong
perimeter. The wrong options are all real security practices, just not the three principles.

### Q29. Which Microsoft service correlates signals across identities, endpoints, email and cloud apps into a unified view?

- [ ] Microsoft Purview
- [x] **Microsoft Defender XDR**
- [ ] Microsoft Intune
- [ ] Microsoft Entra ID

**Why:** **Defender XDR** (extended detection and response) correlates the Defender workload
signals into one incident view, so an alert that touches email, identity and endpoint appears as
a single story rather than three tickets. This is the answer to "the products each see a piece,
what sees the whole?" — and it is why XDR is distinct from SIEM (Sentinel), which aggregates more
broadly including non-Microsoft sources.

### Q30. What is the primary benefit of threat intelligence in Microsoft's security products?

- [ ] It replaces the need for patching
- [x] **It provides current knowledge of known attackers and techniques to prioritise and speed detection**
- [ ] It blocks all malware automatically
- [ ] It reduces licensing costs

**Why:** Threat intelligence supplies **context** — who is likely targeting you, with what
techniques — which lets defenders prioritise. Microsoft feeds its own intelligence into Defender
products, which is why "it is already included" is a reasonable point in its favour. It does not
replace patching, and no intelligence source blocks everything.

### Q31. Which of these is a Microsoft cloud security posture management task rather than a workload protection task?

- [ ] Blocking a malicious process on a virtual machine
- [x] **Identifying an internet-facing storage account that allows public access**
- [ ] Cleaning a malware infection on a laptop
- [ ] Quarantining a phishing email

**Why:** Posture management is about **configuration** — finding misconfigured resources before
anyone exploits them. Blocking a process, cleaning a laptop and quarantining email are all
**runtime protection** of a specific workload. The distinction matters because the two require
different tooling and different teams, and it is directly tested: an exposed storage account is a
configuration finding, and Defender for Cloud is what surfaces it.

## Domain 4 — Capabilities of Microsoft compliance solutions (20–25%)

### Q32. What is a sensitivity label in Microsoft Purview?

- [ ] A password policy
- [x] **A classification applied to content that can carry protection such as encryption and restrictions**
- [ ] A type of user account
- [ ] A backup tag

**Why:** A **sensitivity label** is classification made operational — applying "Confidential" can
automatically encrypt the document and limit who can open or forward it, and the protection travels
with the file even when it leaves your systems. That persistence is the key property: a label is not
just metadata, it can be an enforced control.

### Q33. What does data loss prevention (DLP) do?

- [ ] Prevents disks from failing
- [x] **Detects and blocks the sharing of sensitive information according to policy**
- [ ] Backs up data automatically
- [ ] Encrypts the whole disk

**Why:** **DLP** watches for sensitive data moving where it should not — a credit card number in an
email, a customer list to personal cloud storage — and can warn, block or audit. It is a **Purview**
capability, and it is the answer whenever the scenario involves data leaving rather than an
attacker getting in.

### Q34. What is the purpose of an insider risk management solution?

- [ ] To monitor employee productivity
- [x] **To detect and address risky behaviour by people inside the organisation, such as data exfiltration**
- [ ] To manage visitor access
- [ ] To scan for malware

**Why:** Insider risk is about **people with legitimate access** doing harmful things — deliberately
or accidentally — which is why perimeter controls cannot detect it. Microsoft's Insider Risk
Management correlates signals like mass downloads and unusual sharing. The distinction from DLP
matters: DLP enforces rules on data movement, insider risk *identifies a pattern of behaviour over
time*.

### Q35. What is the difference between a data retention policy and a data deletion policy?

- [ ] There is no difference
- [x] **Retention keeps content for a period; deletion removes it — and both can be scoped by label or location**
- [ ] Retention is for email only
- [ ] Deletion is automatic and retention is manual

**Why:** Both are lifecycle controls in Purview and they answer different requirements — a
regulation may require you to keep records (retention) or to remove them after a period
(deletion). Confusing them is a compliance problem in both directions: keeping data too long is
its own violation. The exam tests the pair together for that reason.

### Q36. What is Microsoft Purview's eDiscovery used for?

- [ ] Recovering deleted files
- [x] **Identifying, collecting and reviewing electronically stored information for legal or investigative purposes**
- [ ] Discovering cloud applications
- [ ] Auditing user permissions

**Why:** **eDiscovery** is the legal-hold and investigation workflow — find the relevant material,
preserve it so it cannot be altered or deleted, and review it. The preservation aspect is what
distinguishes it from a search: once a hold applies, users cannot destroy the content, even
accidentally. This is a compliance capability, not a recovery tool.

### Q37. What does Microsoft Purview Audit provide?

- [ ] Real-time threat blocking
- [x] **A record of user and administrator activities across Microsoft 365 services**
- [ ] Data classification
- [ ] Device management

**Why:** **Audit** is the searchable record of who did what and when — which is what you need
during an investigation or a compliance review. It is evidence, not enforcement. The distinction
worth holding: Audit **records**, DLP **enforces**, sensitivity labels **protect**, and Compliance
Manager **assesses**.

### Q38. Which Microsoft compliance capability helps identify where personal data is stored across your estate?

- [ ] Microsoft Defender for Cloud
- [x] **Microsoft Purview Data Map and information protection**
- [ ] Microsoft Sentinel
- [ ] Microsoft Intune

**Why:** The **Data Map** scans and catalogues data across sources, which is the prerequisite for
any privacy work — you cannot protect or report on personal data you cannot locate. This is the
same "you cannot protect what you cannot see" principle as asset inventory in the networking
papers, applied to data.

### Q39. What is a compliance score in Microsoft Purview Compliance Manager?

- [ ] A user's training completion rate
- [x] **A measurement of progress against recommended improvement actions for the standards you are tracking**
- [ ] The number of audits passed
- [ ] A licence utilisation figure

**Why:** It is a **posture measurement for compliance**, mirroring secure score for security
(Q23). Its value is prioritisation: it tells you which improvement actions would move you furthest
toward a given regulation. It measures progress against recommendations, not a count of completed
audits — and it is not about licences or training, though both are common distractors.

### Q40. An organisation must ensure that deleted content is still recoverable for a legal case.
Which capability applies?

- [ ] Data loss prevention
- [x] **An eDiscovery hold (or a retention policy that preserves the content)**
- [ ] Sensitivity labelling
- [ ] A backup schedule

**Why:** A legal requirement to **preserve** content is met by a hold or a retention policy —
these prevent deletion, which is exactly the requirement. A backup schedule is not a legal
preservation mechanism: backups are operational, may roll off, and are not designed to be
selectively preserved and evidenced. Sensitivity labels protect confidentiality and are unrelated.

## Scoring

**One mark per question, 40 total. No marks deducted for wrong answers — answer everything.**

The pass mark is 700/1000 = **70%**, which is **28 questions**.

| Score | Percentage | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 32–40 | 80–100% | Comfortable pass |
| **28–31** | **70–78%** | **Pass** |
| 27 | 67.5% | Just under the line — a fail |
| 24–26 | 60–65% | Clear fail |
| below 24 | — | Do not book yet |

### If you scored under 28

SC-900 is **breadth over depth**, and the most common failure is knowing security in general but
not knowing which Microsoft product does which job. The fix is product-focused: learn the
**Defender family split** (Cloud, Office 365, Endpoint, Identity, Cloud Apps) and the
**Purview capabilities** (labels, DLP, insider risk, eDiscovery, Audit, Compliance Manager), and
be able to say what each does in one sentence.

If you lost marks on Q17–Q31, that is **38% of this exam** and it is almost entirely product
recognition. Microsoft Learn is free and is the authoritative source for it.

### If you scored 32 or above

This is a fundamentals exam and it is a **starting point**, not a qualification for a security
role. Passing SC-900 says you know Microsoft's security vocabulary. It does not say you can
configure any of it. If your goal is a security job, the hands-on work matters far more: the
curriculum's certification phase makes this argument at length, and it is worth reading before
you let a fundamentals certificate stand in for a lab.
